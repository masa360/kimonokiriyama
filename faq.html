About the uchikake

What is an uchikake?

An uchikake is the lavish outer kimono that a Japanese bride wears at her wedding. It is decorated with gold thread and colorful embroidery, and its long hem spreads out behind her.

Think of it as Japan’s traditional wedding gown.

It began as formal wear for women of samurai families and became established as bridal wear in the Edo period.

How is it different from a yukata or a regular kimono?

An uchikake is a kind of kimono — the one made for brides.

The uchikake is worn as the outermost layer, over a kimono. It is one of the most formal garments in traditional Japanese dress.

How is this different from renting a kimono and walking around town?

Many rental shops let you wear a kimono or yukata around town and return it the same day. Here, you wear a bridal uchikake inside the hotel and enjoy a photo session.

Is it harder to wear than a regular kimono?

No. With the Kimono Experience plan, the uchikake is simply draped over your own clothes, so there is nothing complicated to put on and no kimono knowledge is needed.

With the Full Kimono Dressing plan, our staff dress you step by step in a full kimono. Either way, first-timers are welcome.

Who can join

Can I wear one if I am not Japanese, or not married?

Of course. This is a photo experience, not a wedding, so everyone is welcome, whatever your nationality and whether or not you are married.

Will it fit me if I am tall or petite?

The uchikake is one size fits all, so it suits both tall and petite guests.

If you have any concerns, please mention them in the “Requests or questions” field when you book.

Can men and children join?

Yes. Men wear a formal crested kimono with hakama (Full Kimono Dressing), and children wear a children’s kimono (Kimono Experience).

Children’s kimonos are Shichi-Go-San sizes, so ages 3 to 7 are the guide.

People in the same booking can each be on a different plan.

Can a couple wear it together?

Yes. Women wear the uchikake and men wear the montsuki hakama, a formal crested kimono with hakama trousers, and you can take your photos together.

The uchikake is the most formal attire for a Japanese bride, and the montsuki hakama is the most formal attire for a Japanese groom. Together, they are the formal wear of a Japanese wedding.

Men are dressed in the Full Kimono Dressing plan. Women can choose either plan, and everyone in the same booking can choose their own plan.

You can be dressed at the same time, each in a separate room.

I have never worn a kimono and I do not speak Japanese. Is that OK?

Yes. Our staff will dress you, so first-timers can relax.

Our staff speak Japanese only, and we use translation apps to guide you.

Photos and the day

Can I take photos with my own phone? Can someone take them for me?

Yes, you can use your own smartphone.

A staff member stays with you during the photo session, so feel free to ask them to take the pictures for you (on both plans).

Props are available too: a traditional paper umbrella and a replica sword (not a real blade).

What should I bring?

You can come empty-handed. No special underwear is needed.

Please come straight up to the 4th floor of the hotel by elevator, 10 minutes before your start time. There is no need to stop at the front desk.

Do I need to be staying at the hotel? How do I pay?

No. Guests who are not staying at the hotel are welcome too.

Payment is made on the day at Salon BI-BI on the 4th floor, by cash or credit card (VISA, Mastercard, JCB, AMEX, Diners, Discover, UnionPay).
